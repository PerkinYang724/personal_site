I still remember the first time I came across the words “Artificial Intelligence.” My mind went everywhere. I was going to be a great entrepreneur, an inventor, a creative artist — my imagination was running way ahead of me.

Back then, I had no idea where this thing was heading or how it would shape our lives. But I knew, deep down, that AI was going to change everything. I was convinced that if I just went all in and stayed ahead of everyone else, success would follow.

Turns out… before success ever came, burnout did.

I’m a deeply creative person. The moments I feel most alive are when I’m fully immersed in creation — for me, that’s filmmaking. Scrolling through other creators’ work, digging around the internet for inspiration, getting lost in the process of just *being* and making things. That’s where I feel most human.

When AI started taking off, I dove in headfirst. Deep research agents, MCP, automation workflows — I replaced as many manual tasks as I could. And yeah, my productivity skyrocketed. I was multitasking while agents worked alongside me. This kind of work environment literally didn’t exist before.

But somehow, I felt even more burned out than before.

I stopped cherishing the little moments. I forgot the happiness of writing my first line of code. I forgot what it felt like to finish a simple, kind of boring task and feel that small sense of reward that quietly made my day. The truth is, we lose a lot when technology starts making everything easier. That’s not to say technology is a bad thing — but I think it’s worth pausing and asking ourselves: what should we actually hand off to technology, and what should we keep for ourselves?

This post comes purely from my own experience after two years of living with AI. I was lost then, and honestly, I’m still finding my way now. That’s exactly why I started [Oshen Studio](https://oshenstudio.com) — a creative AI studio and community built to help people find clarity and reclaim their time by using technology more intentionally. I used to define myself as a tech enthusiast. Now, I value human connection more than ever.
