> This is the story of how I went from having nothing to offer the world to producing launch videos for a company that mattered. It started with a camera my father buried on a shelf.

## The System

I used to be the kind of kid the system swallows whole.

In Taiwan, education isn’t about thinking. It’s about conforming. You wake up and fit yourself into a mold that was never made for you. You compress your thoughts. You flatten your voice. You learn to be small enough to disappear. By the time you’re sixteen, you’ve spent nearly a decade learning that the safest place to live is inside the crowd.

I was good at disappearing. Too good.

## The Break

In 2024, before college, I stopped.

I took a full year and did nothing but let my mind settle. No competitions to win. No benchmarks to hit. No one measuring whether I was average or exceptional. Just silence. Just time. And slowly, the noise inside my head began to clear.

“I want to document my life,” I told myself one morning. The words surprised me. They came from somewhere I’d buried so deep I’d almost forgotten it existed.

## The Search

But I had nothing. No camera. No skills. No idea where to begin.

For days, the frustration kept me awake. I lay there thinking about all the things I didn’t know how to do. How to film. How to edit. How to turn raw moments into something worth watching. The questions multiplied until they felt like a wall.

Then something shifted. Instead of asking “How do I become a filmmaker?” I asked “What do I have right now?”

I started searching. Every shelf. Every storage box. Every forgotten corner of the house. I was looking for permission disguised as equipment. Proof that I could start without being ready.

## The Discovery

I found my father’s old Canon 700d buried under a stack of old papers.

It was barely functional. The plastic casing had turned sticky, the kind of decay that happens when eco-friendly materials break down in humidity. The sensor was smeared. Everything about it screamed “broken.” But I held it up to the light and pressed the power button.

It turned on.

That moment mattered more than I knew. A broken tool in my hands was better than no tool at all. The camera wasn’t beautiful or new. It was imperfect and mine. And it was enough to start.

## The Awakening

I began filming everything.

Vlogs. Talking pieces. Reviews of things that didn’t matter. I wasn’t trying to go viral. I wasn’t building an audience. I was just learning to see my own life again, to witness it instead of move through it numb. Each time I pressed record, something inside me came alive. Not because I was good at it. Because I was *doing* it.

For the first time in years, I felt like I had a voice.

## The Refusal

The camera became a permission slip to be alive. It gave me cover to do something the system never asked for: to create. To think. To refuse the script I’d been handed.

I didn’t know where it would lead. I couldn’t imagine producing launch videos for a YC-backed startup. I couldn’t see the agency I’d build, the clients I’d work with, the decisions I’d make that mattered.

I just knew this: I couldn’t go back to being average.

So I picked up that broken camera and started.
